Key Facts About the Milky Way

  1. Galactic Structure: The Milky Way is a barred spiral galaxy defined by a dense, spherical Galactic Bulge at its center, surrounded by a broad Galactic Disc that holds most of the galaxy's gas, dust, and stars including our Sun located in the Orion Arm. Curving outward from the central bar are massive Spiral Arms rich with star-forming regions, while the entire galaxy is wrapped in a vast Galactic Halo filled with ancient globular star clusters and invisible dark matter.
  2. Scale and Composition: Spanning an immense diameter of 100,000 to 150,000 light-years, the Milky Way contains an estimated 100 to 400 billion stars and boasts a total mass roughly 1.5 trillion times that of our Sun. Its physical composition consists mainly of interstellar gas (dominated by hydrogen and helium) and cosmic dust, though the vast majority of its total mass is made up of mysterious, invisible Dark Matter that creates the gravitational framework holding the entire galaxy together.
  3. The Galactic Center: Located about 26,000 light-years from Earth, the Galactic Center is an extremely dense, high-energy core anchored by Sagittarius A*, a supermassive black hole with the mass of roughly 4 million Suns. Because dense clouds of interstellar dust block visible light from reaching Earth, astronomers rely on radio, infrared, and X-ray space telescopes to peer through the galactic shroud and observe the extreme physics taking place around this cosmic anchor.
